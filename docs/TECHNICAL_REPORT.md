# BeliefView: active search under occlusion (synthetic 2D simulation)

A reproducible **synthetic 2D simulation** (not computer vision, not a flight controller, no claim about real drone performance). An observer "drone" loses sight of a moving subject behind opaque cover. It keeps a probability belief over where the subject could be (a grid Bayes filter with an absorbing "outside the search area" state) and uses a **greedy, not globally optimal** viewpoint planner to try to reacquire it. It is compared with simple search patterns on identical, paired, held-out episodes. Ground truth is stored separately and shown only as **evaluation-only ground truth**.

AI assistance: Claude wrote the code (initial implementation by Claude Sonnet 5.5; fix pass, v2 protocol and local demo by Claude Fable 5.2). See `CONTRIBUTIONS.md`. Codex consulted Muse, Grok Bot and Antigravity and recorded their distinct, synthetic-only critiques in the opening reviewer sections of `REVIEW_NOTES.md`; Instinct was not consulted. Codex completed the independent local review; its verdict is recorded in `REVIEW_NOTES.md`.

**Fastest way in:** open `demo/index.html` in a browser (double-click it; no server, login or network). It has both animations with pause, scrub and single-frame steps, the matched-case comparison, the failure, and the limitations, all generated from the v2 result files.

**Two result sets, kept apart.** **v2 is the headline**: travel penalty lam = 0.004, 200 fresh final episodes per scenario, run once (`config/frozen_v2.json`, `results/v2/`). **v1 is an earlier record**: lam = 0 and 100 test episodes per scenario that were run twice (`config/frozen.json`, the files directly in `results/`, and a checksummed copy in `archive/v1/`). They use different episodes and their tables are not merged.

## World, sensor, filter, planner (what the code does)
- **World** (`config.py`, `world.py`): 40x40 map, 4 opaque rectangular obstacles, 1 foliage rectangle, fixed for all episodes. The subject starts hidden behind an obstacle, was last seen with a noisy measurement 15-40 steps before the search starts (the observer knows that age), then follows a pre-rolled behaviour: continue, turn, stop (long or short), double back (scenario B only), conceal (walks to a hiding spot and stays), or exit. It cannot enter obstacles and is absorbed when it crosses the boundary (plus a boundary hazard). Observer: speed 1.5, 50 deg/step turn limit, obstacle-inflated collision-checked controller shared by every policy; the camera points along its heading.
- **Sensor** (`sensor.py`): P(detect) = p0 * exp(-(r/r0)^2) inside a 60-degree FOV wedge, range 12, zero if line of sight is blocked, x0.4 (assumed) in foliage. Line of sight is tested against the physical obstacle rectangles; the rectangles inflated by the drone radius (0.8) are used only for collision checking. Foliage attenuates once, by the subject's own location: a sight line that merely crosses foliage is not attenuated, and a concealing subject gets no additional penalty beyond foliage or opaque cover. There are no false positives. Detections give a Gaussian-noisy position. **Independent looks:** a new look (new detection chance, new miss update) occurs only if the pose changed (0.25 units / 10 deg) or every 3rd step; duplicate steps give no evidence, so staring cannot create false certainty. Looks that do count are treated as independent draws, in the simulator and in the filter alike. This makes moving give more looks per step than staying. That is an assumption that favours movement.
- **Belief** (`belief.py`): b[mode, y, x] with 9 modes (stopped + 8 headings) plus `outside`. Predict = mode transition (stop/resume/turn/reverse) + bilinear advection + small diffusion; mass crossing the boundary, or hit by the assumed exit hazard (0.03/step for moving mass within 2 units of the edge), moves to `outside`; mass that would enter an obstacle cell stays put. Miss update: multiply each state by 1 - P(detect | state, view) (outside has likelihood 1), renormalise. Detection update: multiply by P(detect) x Gaussian measurement likelihood (outside goes to 0). The filter and planner compute visibility from the observer's exact position, as the simulator does; the remaining approximation is that each cell is represented by its centre. Sum of grid + outside = 1 after every step (max error over all test runs 5e-15). The heatmap in the animation shows unconditional per-cell probability on a fixed 0-0.05 scale in every frame, is not renormalised, and the outside probability is a separate bar.
- **Planner** (`planner.py`): candidates = hold position with 8 yaw choices, or travel to a reachable free cell 4/8/12 units away in 8 directions, holding the travel heading or a heading toward the belief centroid. Each is rolled out over 12 steps with the real controller and the predicted belief with expected misses. Score = P(reacquire within 12 steps) - lam x travel steps. In the v2 (headline) run lam = 0.004, so each step of travel costs 0.004 of score; it was chosen on new validation episodes as the largest of {0.0005, 0.001, 0.002, 0.004} whose reacquired fraction was within 0.03 of the grid's best, and its effect is measured against a lam = 0 reference arm (see Results). In the earlier v1 record validation tuning chose lam = 0, so the explicit travel penalty was inactive there; travel still costs look time inside the 12-step horizon. Best single candidate wins, 3 steps are executed, then it replans. At the deadline (80 steps) the run ends unresolved and states P(outside) plus a **conditional in-area 90% region** (the smallest set of cells holding 90% of the belief *given the subject is still inside*; its unconditional probability is about 0.9 x (1 - P(outside)), reported separately); it declares "subject left the area" if P(outside) >= 0.9 (tuned on validation). There is no early stop in v1, hence no wrongful early stops to measure.
- **Baselines** (same map, trajectories, per-step detection uniforms, sensor, look gate, controller, deadline): Stay (camera yaw-scans at 45 deg/step), Last-seen (go to the noisy last-seen point, then scan), Orbit (circle the obstacle nearest the last-seen point, margin 2), and the **no-miss-update ablation**: the same planner with the same settings object (candidate set, horizon, commit length, travel penalty) and controller, differing only in that misses do not update its belief (it still uses detections; nothing is tuned separately for it). Every policy also runs a passive filter so a probability region exists to report; only the two planner policies act on it.
- **Sensing rights:** the simulator applies one look gate to whatever pose a policy reaches, so every policy senses on every new look, including while travelling (tested for all five). In v2, measured looks per step were 1.00 for the three baselines and the lam = 0 reference arm, 0.93 to 0.95 for BeliefView and 0.96 to 0.98 for the ablation, because with a positive travel penalty the planner sometimes holds position. In v1 they were 1.00 for every policy in every scenario.
- **No truth leakage:** inference modules never import `world.py`/`runner.py` (AST test), policies only receive `Observation` objects and the noisy last-seen point/age (the planner rolls the look gate forward from its own copy, built from the assumed sensor and those observations), and a metamorphic test swaps the hidden truth under an identical all-miss observation stream and checks identical paths and beliefs.

## Evaluation design
Seeds: `SeedSequence([base, scenario, split, episode])`. Split ids: dev = 0 (development and unit tests), val = 1 and test = 2 (v1), **v2_val = 3 and v2_final = 4 (v2; never used by any earlier run)**.

**v2 protocol (headline; fixed in `DEMO_PASS_PLAN.md` before any v2 episode existed).** Map, scenarios, sensor, subject model, planner candidates, horizon and commit length, orbit margin 2, scan yaw 45 degrees per step and the outside threshold 0.9 are the v1 values and were not re-tuned. Two things changed: the travel-penalty selection rule and the episode streams. One shared, strictly positive lam was tuned for BeliefView on 100 new validation episodes in each of scenarios A and B (split id 3) from the grid {0.0005, 0.001, 0.002, 0.004}, taking the largest value whose combined reacquired fraction was within 0.03 absolute of the grid's best. The ablation inherits that lam. The config, protocol, seed mapping and a checksum of the simulation and inference source were then frozen (`config/frozen_v2.json`: config hash `a69bfac2342ac108`, protocol hash `005759e5dd0fd210`, core source checksum `964103366f5b5762`), and the final run was made **once** on **200 fresh final episodes per scenario** (split id 4), the same episodes for every policy (paired). BeliefView with lam = 0 was run on those same final episodes only as a labelled paired **reference arm**; it was never a tuning candidate and is not a headline result. A lock file blocks a second inferential run. Nothing was changed in response to the v2 final results; any substantive correction would need a separately labelled v3 with new seeds.

What is paired, how reacquisition is defined, the four scenarios and the statistics are the same in v1 and v2: what is paired is the episode (trajectory, start, last-seen measurement) and the pre-drawn per-step detection uniforms and measurement noise. The observations are not identical across policies: a detection needs the shared uniform to fall below the detection probability at that policy's own viewpoint. Scenarios: A matched; B motion mismatch (long stops of 50-120 steps, doubling back); C sensor mismatch (true foliage factor 0.10 vs assumed 0.4, true p0 0.75 vs 0.9, true range scale 7 vs assumed 6, true measurement noise 1.0 vs 0.6); D exit-heavy truth (hazard 0.08, 35% exit-bound) with an assumed-exit-rate sweep. Reacquisition = first positive detection (the sim has no false positives). Searches that hit the deadline are **censored at T=80** and counted as unresolved; search time is reported as restricted-mean search time (RMST, timeouts counted as 80). Intervals are 95% percentile bootstraps over episodes; differences are paired. Intervals are not adjusted for the number of comparisons.

**v1 design (earlier record), as originally written:** Seeds: `SeedSequence([base, scenario, split, episode])`; splits dev (development/tests), val (tuning), test (final) are disjoint. Hyperparameters were tuned on 40 validation episodes in each of scenarios A and B (small grids: one planner lam shared by BeliefView and the ablation, orbit margin, scan rate, tau_left), frozen with a config hash (`222bce7e37097894`), then run on **100 held-out test episodes per scenario**, the same episodes for all five policies (paired). What is paired is the episode (trajectory, start, last-seen measurement) and the pre-drawn per-step detection uniforms and measurement noise. The observations are not identical across policies: a detection needs the shared uniform to fall below the detection probability at that policy's own viewpoint. These test episodes were also run once by the initial implementation, before the fix pass changed the code, so this is their second use. Scenarios: A matched; B motion mismatch (long stops of 50-120 steps, doubling back); C sensor mismatch (true foliage factor 0.10 vs assumed 0.4, true p0 0.75 vs 0.9, true range scale 7 vs assumed 6, true measurement noise 1.0 vs 0.6); D exit-heavy truth (hazard 0.08, 35% exit-bound) with an assumed-exit-rate sweep. Conditional in-area 90% region at the deadline (BeliefView): it holds 90% of the belief given the subject is still inside, but only 0.19 to 0.23 of the total probability, because most of the mass is on "outside". By behaviour (see `results/v2/results.md`; small-n rows flagged): all policies find concealed and stopped subjects most often and rarely find exiting ones (as expected, they leave). Differences between BeliefView and Last-seen by behaviour are inside the noise of 15-84 episodes per cell.

## Failures and honest limitations
These bullets describe v2 unless they say v1.
- **A modest result:** what the v2 data support is: BeliefView beats staying still in every scenario and a fixed orbit in three of four (A, B, D; not under sensor mismatch) with about half the orbit's travel; it beats last-seen-then-scan clearly in the matched scenario and narrowly in the exit-heavy one, and is indistinguishable from it under motion mismatch and sensor mismatch, while flying about twice as far in all four.
- **Miss updates did not show a benefit in v2.** The clean ablation's reacquisition differences are +0.01 to +0.03 with every interval including 0. The few-point benefit that v1 reported in two scenarios did not hold up on fresh episodes. Updating on misses is the idea this project was built around, and on this evidence its effect on reacquisition is too small to detect with 200 episodes per scenario; it only shortened travel by about 4 to 6.5 units.
- **Model-mismatch failure (scenario B):** under mismatched motion BeliefView matched the last-seen baseline (0.68 vs 0.67, difference +0.01 [-0.03, +0.05]) at 1.8 times the travel, and its deadline report was unreliable: 2 false "left" calls among 9 still-inside unresolved runs, and only 16 correct "left" calls for 56 subjects that had left. `results/v2/animation_failure.mp4` (scenario B, final episode 0) was picked by a fixed rule (the first scenario B timeout whose subject was still inside). It shows a plain miss: the subject kept walking, was still inside at the deadline and was never seen, with final P(outside) 0.30 and no "left" call. It is an illustrative episode selected by outcome, not an example of the false-"left" failure. False "left" calls also occurred in 4 of 11 still-inside cases in the matched scenario and 5 of 14 under sensor mismatch. Still-inside cases are rare in the unresolved set, so these rates are not well estimated. (v1's failure clip, `results/animation_failure.mp4`, scenario B test episode 20, did show a false "left": P(outside) 0.98 with the subject inside.)
- **Many subjects leave:** 97 to 140 of the 200 subjects per scenario leave the map before the deadline (113 in A, 97 in B, 121 in C, 140 in D). This caps every method's reacquisition rate, follows from my map size and speed choices, and means most unresolved runs are true exits.
- **No false positives:** the simulated sensor never reports a subject that is not there, so the first detection is always correct. Real detectors are not like this, and nothing here measures how the filter or planner would cope.
- Why last-seen is strong here: subjects stay within a few units of where they vanished (speed 0.3, search starts 15-40 steps later) and a scanning camera covers that neighbourhood. A different map, faster subject or longer delay could change the ranking. I did not test those.
- The look gate rewards movement: any move of 0.25 units or 10 degrees earns a fresh independent look. With lam = 0 (v1, and the v2 reference arm) the planner travelled on almost every step. With lam = 0.004 it sometimes holds position (0.93 to 0.95 looks per step) and flies a quarter to a third less. A sensor whose successive looks are correlated would change this.
- **The travel penalty is a trade, chosen by a rule, on a small grid.** The v2 rule deliberately accepts up to 0.03 less validation reacquisition for less travel, and it picked the largest value on the grid (0.004), so nothing larger was tried. On the final episodes the penalty saved 12 to 20 units of travel per episode; reacquisition was not distinguishable from the lam = 0 reference arm in three scenarios and was possibly lower under sensor mismatch (-0.040 [-0.085, +0.000]). The validation differences among lam values were within noise (0.605 to 0.620 of 200). In v1, validation chose lam = 0 by one episode in 80, so v1's travel penalty was inactive.
- **Intervals are unadjusted.** Many paired comparisons are reported. Marginal intervals (BeliefView vs Last-seen in D, vs Orbit in B) could be chance.
- **Use of the evaluation episodes.** The v2 final episodes (split id 4) were run once, after the config, protocol and source checksum were frozen, and no setting was changed afterwards. The v1 test episodes were run twice (before and after the fix pass) and a third time in this pass as a labelled reproduction check; v1 is an earlier record, not the headline. The v2 scenarios, map and everything except lam and the seeds were designed while looking at v1-era results, so v2 is a fresh sample from the same simulated world, not an independent replication of the design.
- Simulation-vs-reality: abstract 2D rectangles, perfect self-pose, no perception errors or false positives, no altitude, camera projection, gimbal, wind, latency, battery, or real control dynamics (one speed, one turn limit, unit time steps), one fixed map, hand-written subject behaviours, a sensor model that is an assumption (including the independent-look gate). Nothing here predicts real drone performance, and nothing here is an operational system.
- A learned motion prior and a particle filter were deliberately not built.

## Related work
These papers motivated questions and limitations. None of them validates BeliefView's numbers, and none is a drone-tracking result. Notes are in `LITERATURE_NOTES.md`.
- Chen and Kurniawati, ["POMDP Planning for Object Search in Partially Unknown Environment"](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/a6d7226db2ff3643d8624624e3859c19-Paper-Conference.pdf) (NeurIPS 2023). A Fetch mobile manipulator searches indoors for static objects occluded by furniture in a partially known 3D scene, using a growing-state POMDP solved with GPOMCP; it can move its base, head and lift and remove occluders, and is evaluated in four Gazebo scenarios. BeliefView shares only the broad idea of acting on an uncertain target location to improve visibility. Here the subject moves, the map is known and 2D, the observer cannot manipulate cover, and the planner is a small greedy rollout, not a POMDP solver.
- Taioli et al., ["Unsupervised Active Visual Search with Monte Carlo Planning under Uncertain Detections"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.03155) (2023). Indoor object search on a 2D floor map with a real RGB-D detector, belief updates and POMCP-style planning. Relevant to treating a miss as uncertain evidence. It handles false positives; BeliefView's sensor has none.
- Velez et al., ["Active Exploration for Robust Object Detection"](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2011/458) (IJCAI 2011). A mobile sensor weighs observation detours against motion cost and models correlation between successive observations. This is the motivation for the travel penalty and the explicit repeated-look rule; its correlation model is richer than the fixed look gate here.
- Chung and Silvestrini, ["Modeling and Analysis of Exhaustive Probabilistic Search"](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/nav.21574) (Naval Research Logistics 2014). An analytic Bayesian presence/absence search for one static target with imperfect detection. This is why outside decisions and false declarations are reported separately here.

## What not to claim
Don't say it beats simple search generally, that it "finds people faster", that the planner is optimal, that it is an operational system, or anything about real drones. Say what the confidence intervals support (see 30-second description).
